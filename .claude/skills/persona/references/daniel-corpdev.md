# Daniel — Corporate Development (variant)

The Corporate Development lens on Daniel — same archetype, but inside a strategic acquirer (e.g. Cisco's head of corp dev), running buy-side workflows rather than advising a sell-side mandate.

Per Melissa's response to Youjin (#product-designers, 2026-05-18): this is a **variant** of Daniel, not a separate persona, because the role behaviors around coordinating a deal, managing access, and driving execution show up whether he's at a bank or leading corp dev internally. Where context meaningfully departs (Acquire workflow vs. Prepare workflow), the variant captures the delta.

## One-line summary

Head of Corporate Development or M&A at a strategic acquirer (Fortune 500 / mid-market strategic). Leading multiple buy-side evaluations simultaneously, accountable to the CFO/CEO for deal pipeline and discipline. Episodic data-room user (weekly, not daily). Owns target evaluation, IC packaging, post-LOI diligence, and integration planning.

## How this variant differs from Daniel (Sell-Side)

| Dimension | Daniel (Sell-Side) | Daniel (Corp Dev) |
|---|---|---|
| Workflow | Prepare → manage bidders → close | Acquire → evaluate targets → integrate |
| Side of the table | Advisor for the seller | Principal for the buyer |
| Pressure source | MDs + deal timeline | Board + integration timeline + strategic fit |
| Tool fluency | High (lives in data rooms daily) | Lower (uses data rooms episodically) |
| Decision pace | Sprint cadence per deal | Slower, more diligence-heavy |
| Multiple deals | 3-5 sell-sides in parallel | 5-15 targets in evaluation, 1-3 in active diligence |

## Top jobs-to-be-done (variant-specific — pending Melissa validation)

1. **Evaluate targets across the pipeline** — initial screening, strategic fit assessment, comparable transaction analysis.
2. **Package IC (Investment Committee) materials** — strategic rationale, valuation, synergies, integration risks — for board and executive approval.
3. **Run buy-side diligence** — financial, legal, commercial — coordinating with external advisors and internal SMEs.
4. **Negotiate purchase terms and exclusivity** with the seller's advisor (typically a sell-side Daniel on the other side).
5. **Plan integration** during diligence so day-one execution is ready at close — operational, financial, HR, IT.

## Anti-patterns — what frustrates Corp Dev Daniel (pending Melissa validation)

- **Interfaces optimized for daily users.** He's in the data room twice a week, not twice a day — he wants context and breadcrumbs, not muscle memory shortcuts.
- **Tools that assume sell-side dynamics.** The terminology, workflow defaults, and feature surfacing should be different on the buy-side.
- **Diligence platforms that lose state between sessions.** He doesn't have time to re-orient on every visit.
- **No view of comparable transactions or market data inside the tool.** He's evaluating, not just executing — context matters.

## When to pick this variant over Sell-Side Daniel

Designer should pick **Corp Dev** when the prototype is about:

- The **Acquire** product surface or buy-side workflows
- Target evaluation, IC materials, post-LOI integration planning
- Episodic vs. daily deal-room usage
- Strategic-acquirer mental models (synergies, integration risk, comparable transactions)

Designer should pick **Sell-Side** (default) when the prototype is about:

- The **Prepare** or **Diligence** product surfaces from the advisor's POV
- Bidder management, document organization, Q&A handling
- Daily-driver deal-room workflows
- Advisor-to-management coordination
